The beaches

Snells Beach faces east across Kawau Bay. It is a flat swim, best about two hours either side of high. A fresh easterly blows it out.

Algies Bay is the calm bay just south of Snells. The sand is good for about three hours either side of high, and it is not worth it at low.

Scotts Landing is on the harbour side at the south end of the peninsula. It is the shelter when a northerly is up, and an easterly is blocked by the land too. A couple of hours either side of high.

Martins Bay is the more open local bay, but it is all-tide, and it can pick up a little for a bodyboard.

Scandretts Bay is a small beach on the northeast tip, with rock pools at low tide. It is not surf.

Baddeleys and Campbells sit on the south side of Tāwharanui, so a northerly is offshore and a southerly is in their face. They are high-tide swims. The road goes through Matakana.

Omaha and Anchor Bay at Tāwharanui face northeast. Pakiri and Te Arai are more open, cleanest in a west to southwest with swell from the northeast. Te Arai gets the most size, and Tāwharanui the least. All of them go through Matakana.

Mathesons Bay at Leigh is a sheltered swim at any tide.

Goat Island is the snorkel. Skip it in an east or northeast over about 20 knots, or swell over a metre from that direction.

Sandspit is the ferry, not a swim.

On Saturday and Sunday the Matakana road is the queue for Omaha, Tāwharanui, Leigh, Pakiri, Te Arai, Baddeleys, and Campbells. Snells, Algies, Martins, and Scotts do not go through it.
